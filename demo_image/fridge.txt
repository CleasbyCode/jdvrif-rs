The 0–5 dial is a cooling power or intensity scale, not a direct temperature setting. 

Higher numbers mean the compressor works harder and longer to remove heat, resulting in a colder fridge. 

A setting of 2 provides minimal cooling power (leading to an unsafe 11°C), while 5 provides maximum power to reach a safe 4°C.

How the Dial Works

0: The cooling system is turned off.

1–2: Low cooling power, minimal compressor runtime (results in a warmer interior).

3–4: Medium power, usually ideal for normal room temperatures and full loads.

5: Maximum cooling power, continuous or heavy compressor runtime (achieves your ideal 4°C).

Why Setting 2 Leaves Your Fridge at 11°C 

Ambient temperature: If your kitchen or the room where the fridge sits is warm, a low power setting (2) 
cannot pull enough heat out of the cabinet.

Under-loading or airflow: A very empty fridge loses its "cold mass" every time the door opens, and a low setting lacks the power to recover quickly.

Thermostat calibration: The internal thermostat sensor may be slightly lazy or miscalibrated, meaning it requires a high physical setting (5) just to keep the compressor running long enough to hit a proper food-safe zone below 5°C.
